# S1.3 — Authorization via AAD groups

## In one line
SDM decides what you can see and do in **two layers**: your **menu options** come from your **Entra (Azure AD) `DA-*` group membership** (the groups that ride along on your sign-in token), and the **data on those screens** is then **filtered by the Permission tables in DDH's `Security` schema** — the full **RACI-applied** access.

## The idea
When you open SDM you sign in through **Entra ID using OpenID Connect (OIDC)**. The token Entra returns carries your **group claims** — specifically the **`DA-*` security groups** (e.g. `DA-FNC-CCSD-ADMSDM` for admins, `DA-SCH-…` for school-level roles).

Those `DA-*` claims drive the **menu**: SDM decides which options to make available by reading the groups off your token. The `DA-*` memberships themselves aren't SDM's to *manage* — that access is **system-generated by `Sync-DDH-AAD`**, the sync that aggregates the underlying data and writes the Azure `DA-*` groups. What SDM does — true to its supplemental nature — is **augment** that system-generated access. The **Standalone Group** menu, for instance, lets the Help Desk add a person to a `DA-*`-linked group to supply access, including **temporary** access with an end date. That edit is **saved to DDH**; `Sync-DDH-AAD` then picks it up, aggregates it, and syncs it to the Azure `DA-*` groups, so it reaches the user's token at their **next sign-in**. In short: **SDM supplies the input; `Sync-DDH-AAD` generates the access.**

Authorization then works at two layers:
1. **The menu — from `DA-*` groups.** SDM builds your navigation from your group claims. Internally each link maps to a **"management task,"** and a task only appears if one of your `DA-*` groups grants it. Two people can open SDM and see **different menus**. At the API, every `/api/*` call re-checks your groups; if you lack the required `DA-*` group, the backend returns **403 Forbidden**.
2. **The data — filtered by the DDH `Security`-schema Permission tables.** Once a screen opens, the **data** it shows is filtered by the **Permission tables in DDH's `Security` schema**, which apply the **full RACI** model. So two users with the same menu can still see **different rows/records**, according to what those permissions grant them.

So a `403` in SDM almost always means one thing: **you're missing the `DA-*` group** for that area — not that the feature is broken (and that group can often be granted via Standalone Groups). If instead you *can* open a screen but the **data looks thin or absent**, that's the second layer — the **DDH `Security`-schema permissions** filtering what you're entitled to see.

There's also session handling around this: after sign-in the backend keeps a session cookie (idle timeout, default 1200s) and does **silent token refresh** so you aren't re-prompted constantly. For local dev, `appsettings.json` has **impersonation** settings (a username + AAD groups) so a developer can test *as* a given role without that person signing in — dev-only.

## Why it matters
Almost every "why can't I see X?" question splits along the two layers. A **403 / a missing menu option** traces back to **group membership** — look at the user's `DA-*` groups, and often *fix* it by supplying the missing membership via the **Standalone Group** menu (knowing `Sync-DDH-AAD` is what actually propagates it into the Azure group, and the change only lands on the user's **next sign-in**). But **"I can open the screen, yet the data is missing/thin"** is a *different* problem — that's the **Permission tables in DDH's `Security` schema** (the RACI-applied access) filtering what the user is entitled to see. Knowing which layer you're in tells you exactly where to look.

## Watch out for
- **"Authorization is just the `DA-*` groups."** That's only the **menu** layer. The **data** on a screen is separately filtered by the **Permission tables in DDH's `Security` schema** — the full **RACI-applied** access. Same menu, different permissions → different data.
- **"The permissions table lives inside SDM."** No — those Permission tables are in **DDH's `Security` schema**, not in SDM. SDM reads your `DA-*` claims for the menu and relies on the DDH `Security`-schema permissions to filter the screen data.
- **"SDM manages user access."** Not quite — the `DA-*` access is **system-generated by `Sync-DDH-AAD`**. SDM **augments** it: a human supplies supplemental input (e.g. Standalone-Group membership), saved to DDH, that `Sync-DDH-AAD` then propagates into the Azure `DA-*` groups. SDM is the supplemental editor, not the system that grants the access.
- **"You can't change access from within SDM."** You *can influence* it — the **Standalone Group** menu is how the Help Desk supplies `DA-*` membership for temporary access. But you're feeding the sync, not flipping a switch in SDM: the authorization decision still **reads the claims** on your token, which `Sync-DDH-AAD` populates.
- **"A new permission takes effect immediately."** No — SDM authorizes from the `DA-*` claims already on the token. A membership you supply is **saved to DDH**, propagated to the Azure `DA-*` groups by `Sync-DDH-AAD`, and only applies once the user **signs in again**.

## You've got this when you can…
- State the **two layers**: menus come from **Entra `DA-*` group claims** (delivered via OIDC); the data on those screens is filtered by the **Permission tables in DDH's `Security` schema** — the full **RACI-applied** access.
- Explain why two users see different menus (their groups grant different management tasks) — and why two users with the *same* menu can still see different data (the `Security`-schema permissions).
- Diagnose a `403` as a **missing AAD group**, and know the membership can be supplied in SDM via the **Standalone Group** menu, then propagated by `Sync-DDH-AAD` (effective at next sign-in) — while thin/absent *data* on an accessible screen points to the **DDH `Security`-schema permissions** instead.
- State the access model correctly: the `DA-*` access is **system-generated by `Sync-DDH-AAD`**; SDM doesn't *manage* it, it **augments** it. SDM's two jobs here are to **read** `DA-*` claims for authorization decisions and to **supply supplemental membership** (Standalone Groups) that the sync propagates. Impersonation is the dev-only way to test as a role.
