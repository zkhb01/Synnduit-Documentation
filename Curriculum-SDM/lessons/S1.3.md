# S1.3 — Authorization via AAD groups

## In one line
SDM decides what you can see and do from your **Entra (Azure AD) group membership** — the `DA-*` groups that ride along on your sign-in token.

## The idea
When you open SDM you sign in through **Entra ID using OpenID Connect (OIDC)**. The token Entra returns carries your **group claims** — specifically the **`DA-*` security groups** (e.g. `DA-FNC-CCSD-ADMSDM` for admins, `DA-SCH-…` for school-level roles).

For an authorization *decision*, SDM simply **reads** those claims off your token — it doesn't compute permissions from a settings table. The group **memberships** themselves come from two places: some are maintained upstream by `Sync-DDH-AAD`; others are managed **in SDM itself** — the **Standalone Group** menu lets the Help Desk add or remove `DA-*` membership to grant access, including **temporary** access. A change made there is **saved to DDH**; `Sync-DDH-AAD` then aggregates that data and syncs it to the Azure `DA-*` groups, so it reaches the user's token at their **next sign-in**.

Authorization then works at two layers:
1. **The menu.** SDM builds your navigation from your group claims. Internally each link maps to a **"management task,"** and a task only appears if one of your `DA-*` groups grants it. Two people can open SDM and see **different menus**.
2. **The API.** Every `/api/*` call re-checks your groups. If you lack the required `DA-*` group, the backend returns **403 Forbidden**.

So a `403` in SDM almost always means one thing: **you're missing the AAD group** for that area — not that the feature is broken (and that group can often be granted via Standalone Groups).

There's also session handling around this: after sign-in the backend keeps a session cookie (idle timeout, default 1200s) and does **silent token refresh** so you aren't re-prompted constantly. For local dev, `appsettings.json` has **impersonation** settings (a username + AAD groups) so a developer can test *as* a given role without that person signing in — dev-only.

## Why it matters
Almost every "why can't I see X?" or "why am I getting a 403?" question traces back to group membership. Knowing that authorization is **claims-from-AAD-groups** tells you exactly where to look — the user's `DA-*` groups — and often where to *fix* it: grant the missing group via the **Standalone Group** menu, remembering the change only lands on the user's next sign-in.

## Watch out for
- **"You can't change access from within SDM."** You can — the **Standalone Group** menu manages `DA-*` group membership (it's how the Help Desk grants temporary access). What SDM *doesn't* do is compute permissions from a settings table; the authorization decision still **reads the claims** on your token.
- **"A new permission takes effect immediately."** No — SDM authorizes from the `DA-*` claims already on the token. A membership you grant is **saved to DDH**, propagated to the Azure `DA-*` groups by `Sync-DDH-AAD`, and only applies once the user **signs in again**.

## You've got this when you can…
- Say that SDM authorizes from **Entra `DA-*` group claims** delivered via OIDC.
- Explain why two users see different menus (their groups grant different management tasks).
- Diagnose a `403` as a **missing AAD group**, and know it can be granted in SDM via the **Standalone Group** menu (effective at next sign-in).
- Distinguish SDM's two jobs here: it **reads** `DA-*` claims to make authorization decisions, and it **manages** some of those group memberships (Standalone Groups). Impersonation is the dev-only way to test as a role.
