# S3.13 — Team Site Security

## In one line
Team Site Security is where you **review and maintain** who can access which sites — and you can look at the same access two ways: **by asset** or **by user**.

## The idea
This is the surface for keeping team site access in order. The underlying information is one thing — the per-site access relationship between **sites/assets** and the **users** who can reach them (the access concept from S1.4). What changes is the **lens** you view it through:

- **By asset** — start from a **site/asset** and see **which users** can access it.
- **By user** — start from a **person** and see **which sites** they can access.

Same access matrix, two entry points. Which view you pick depends on the question you're answering: "who can get into this site?" leads you to the by-asset view; "what can this person reach?" leads you to the by-user view.

## Why it matters
When you're checking or fixing access, picking the right lens gets you there faster. If someone asks "should this person still be able to see that site?" you can confirm it from either direction — but starting from the user (by user) or from the site (by asset) that the question is phrased around saves you hunting. Knowing both views describe the *same* underlying access also keeps you from thinking they're two separate things to maintain.

## Watch out for
**Thinking by-asset and by-user are different data.** They aren't — they're two presentations of the **same** per-site access. A change visible one way is the same change visible the other way; you're not maintaining two lists.

## You've got this when you can…
- State that Team Site Security is the **review-and-maintain** surface for team site access (the access concept from S1.4).
- Explain the two lenses: **by asset** (a site → its users) and **by user** (a person → their sites).
- Pick the right view for a question — "who can access this site?" (by asset) vs. "what can this person access?" (by user) — knowing both show the same access.
