# S1.1 — What SDM is

## In one line
SDM is the human, self-serve editor where data owners **add and maintain, in DDH, the supplemental attributes** that downstream systems need, but the source systems don't provide.

## The idea
The name is the job: **Supplemental Data Manager**. Its core role — from the start and still today — is to take the data imported from **systems of record** and **augment it, in DDH, with the extra attributes downstream apps need that the source systems don't supply**. It began as a way to aggregate the small pockets of district data needed to complement those downstream systems, surfaced through data-driven exports from the **District Data Hub (DDH)**.

It's an **evolved** application — its scope widened over time:
- The volatile **template and configuration JSON** files that drive the syncs were moved into SDM.
- It took on staffing categories **HR doesn't manage** — temporary jobs (under six weeks), **contractors** (a group that also covers student teachers and volunteers), and **vendors** (3rd-party suppliers).

Throughout, it's designed around a **RACI self-serve model** (a Responsibility Assignment Matrix): editorial responsibility sits with the people who *own* the data — the data-owners team and delegated school staff — rather than a central team keying everything in.

That evolution is why SDM is **now** the day-to-day administrative editor for the CSSD-curated portion of DDH: schools, school staff, district programs, departments, municipal divisions, security groups, standalone groups, trustee positions, vendor reps, wards, site security, and more. The domain it edits is the same domain the `Sync-*` family reads and writes mechanically — but where the syncs reconcile DDH with HR / PowerSchool / Entra / SharePoint automatically, **SDM is where humans make the authoritative editorial calls** that drive what those syncs propagate.

## Why it matters
Two ideas anchor everything else. First, **"supplemental"**: SDM isn't just a window onto DDH — it *adds and maintains the attributes the source systems never provided*, stored **in DDH** alongside the imported records (plus the config that steers the syncs). Second, **self-serve ownership**: the right person to fix a school's data is the person who owns it, not a central queue. Miss either and you'll misjudge what SDM is for.

## Watch out for
- **"It's just another sync tool."** No — the mechanical movement between HR, Entra, AD, and SharePoint is the `Sync-*` fleet's job. SDM is where a **person** makes a deliberate edit.
- **"The data SDM adds lives somewhere outside DDH."** No — the supplemental attributes SDM maintains are stored **in DDH**, layered onto the imported system-of-record data. SDM keeps no data of its own; it's the *editor*, and DDH holds the result.
- **"You create master records like schools in SDM."** No. Schools (and permanent staff) come from a **system of record** — the syncs load them into DDH. SDM **augments** those records with supplemental attributes; it can't create or delete them. It *does* create the things it owns: standalone groups, the HR-gap staffing (temp jobs, contractors, vendors), and data-owner assignments.

- Describe the **RACI self-serve model** — data owners edit their own data.
- Give examples of SDM's widened scope (sync template/config JSON; temp jobs under six weeks, contractors, vendors).
- Distinguish what SDM **augments** (system-of-record records like schools — never created or deleted here) from what it **owns** (standalone groups, temp/contractor staffing, data-owner assignments).
- Contrast SDM (human editorial calls) with the mechanical `Sync-*` fleet.
