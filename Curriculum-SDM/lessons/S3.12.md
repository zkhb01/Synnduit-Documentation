# S3.12 — Trustees and Wards

## In one line
Two sibling pages describe CSSD's elected governance: **Trustee Positions** maintains the 7 elected trustees with their position and boundary maps, and **Wards** maintains the set of wards and each ward's relationship to its elected trustee.

## The idea
CSSD is governed by **7 elected trustees**, and SDM holds the data that describes that governance — split across two related pages:

- **Trustee Positions** — maintain the 7 elected trustees, each with its **position** and its **boundary map**.
- **Wards** — maintain the **set of wards**, and the **relationship of each ward to the elected trustee** who represents it.

The two are siblings: a trustee position covers a boundary, and the wards inside that boundary are the ones tied back to that trustee. This is **org-structure** data (S1.4), not people-and-roles HR data — you're describing seats and the geography they cover.

A concrete CSSD example: when the district redraws its electoral map, you update the **boundary maps** on the affected trustee positions, then on the **Wards** page re-point each moved ward to the trustee who now represents it — so a parent looking up "who is my trustee?" resolves to the correct person.

This is one of the entities whose **primary consumer is the external district site** — the public-facing site reads this trustee-and-ward data to show residents their elected representatives. Like all SDM edits (S1.4), changes write to DDH with `CreatedBy`/`UpdatedBy` audit.

## Why it matters
This data is published externally, so a wrong ward-to-trustee link or a stale boundary map shows up on the public district site — residents could be pointed at the wrong representative. Knowing that Trustee Positions and Wards are two halves of the same governance picture tells you to keep them in step: a boundary change on a position usually means a ward relationship has to move too.

## Watch out for
**Treating trustees like staff, or treating the two pages as unrelated.** Trustee Positions are **elected seats** (there are exactly 7) — they belong to org structure, not to the Staff/HR people-and-roles area (S1.4). And Wards aren't standalone: each ward carries a **relationship to its elected trustee**, so editing one page without checking the other leaves the governance map inconsistent.

## You've got this when you can…
- State that Trustee Positions maintains the **7 elected trustees** with their **position and boundary maps**, and that Wards maintains the **set of wards** and **each ward's relationship to its trustee**.
- Explain that this trustee-and-ward data is used for the **external district site**.
- Recognize that the two pages are siblings — a boundary change on a trustee position usually means re-pointing wards — and that, like all edits, changes write to DDH with audit (S1.4).
